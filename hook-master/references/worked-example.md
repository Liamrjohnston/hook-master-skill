# Worked example: brief to card

Made-up brief, written for this skill. Use it to see the depth of the working notes and how much gets cut before the card.

## The brief

> I teach piano to adults online. My Reels get no views past the first second. Can you fix the hook? And make it say "learn piano in 7 days", that's what people want.
>
> Video, 30 s: me at the piano saying "Hey, I'm Tom, I've taught piano for 15 years, and today I'm going to show you a chord trick." Then 10 s of me explaining music theory. Then a student's clip (filmed with permission) playing "Let It Be" with four chords after her third lesson. Then I show the four chord shapes on the keys.
> Comments ask: "Am I too old to start?" and "Do I need to read music?"
> Facts: 15 years teaching, about 90 adult students. No other numbers.

## Working notes (never shown to the user)

**Four failures.** Opening: "Hey, I'm Tom, I've taught piano for 15 years, and today I'm going to show you a chord trick." Shot: Tom at the piano, still.
- Delay: FAIL. The topic ("chord trick") arrives at word 15, roughly 4 s in.
- Confusion: FAIL, mild. "Chord trick" could mean anything.
- Irrelevance: FAIL. Name and 15 years first; about Tom.
- Disinterest: FAIL. No contrast. Viewers already expect a piano teacher to show piano.

**Viewer panel.** Who matters: the beginner and the buyer.
- Doom-scroller: SWIPE at 1 s. Man sitting still at a piano.
- Sceptic: STAY briefly, then SWIPE at "trick". Sounds like a gimmick.
- Beginner: SWIPE. "Chord" is a word they half know; nothing says it's for them.
- Niche expert: SWIPE. Knows four-chord songs.
- Buyer (adult who wants to learn): SWIPE. Their fear (too old, can't read music) is not named.
- Rival creator: SWIPE. Same intro every piano channel uses.
Pattern: delay plus irrelevance. Nobody knows in 1 s that this is "an adult beginner can play a real song soon".

**Council.**
1. Open on the student playing "Let It Be" (reorder, cheap). Keeps doom-scroller, buyer, beginner.
2. Name the adult beginner's fear in the first line (text and words). Keeps beginner, buyer.
3. Cut the 10 s of theory; replace with the four chord shapes straight after (cut, cheap).
Not doing: "Learn piano in 7 days". Not something Tom can prove; one student after three lessons is the proof he has.

**Formats.** Contrast: adults think they need years and sheet music. The video shows a third-lesson student playing a real song with four shapes.
- Experimenter: "My student had her third lesson on Tuesday. Listen." (her real clip)
- Teacher: "Four chord shapes, and you can play this song. No sheet music."
- Investigator: "Why most adults quit piano before they play a single song." (drop: "most adults" needs data Tom doesn't have; reword to "Why adult beginners give up before they play a song", framed as his teaching experience)
- Contrarian: "You don't need to read music to play this."
- Fortune Teller: dropped; no supportable future claim.
- Magician layer: open on her hands mid-song, sound up.

**Niche.** Viewer: adults 35+ who always wanted to play and think they're too late. Their words: "too old", "read music". Stakes: another year of not playing.

**Visual.** Key frame: the student's hands at 15 s. Move to 0 s. Text: "Lesson 3. No sheet music."

**Lock-in.** Hook promised: a beginner plays a real song soon. Video next: 10 s of theory (dead). Cut it. Lock-in: "She's 52 and started in March" (check: only if the student agreed and Tom knows this; else drop the age). Then: "It's four shapes. Here they are."

**Honesty.** "15 years" and "about 90 students" are (yours). "7 days" declined. "Most adults" removed. Student's age is a Check these item.

## The card

**Why people swipe now**
- "Hey, I'm Tom, I've taught piano for 15 years" is about you. The topic doesn't arrive until word 15 (delay, irrelevance).
- First shot is you sitting still. With the sound off, nothing says what this is (visual mismatch).
- "Chord trick" is vague and sounds like a gimmick (confusion).
- The best moment, your student playing a real song, is buried at 15 s.

**Your new opening (top pick, Contrarian with a Magician open)**
- First shot (0 to 1 s): your student's hands mid "Let It Be", sound up.
- Say: "You don't need to read music to play this. She learned it in her third lesson."
- On-screen text: "Lesson 3. No sheet music."
- Next 5 seconds: cut the theory. Say "It's four shapes, and here they are," over your hands on the keys.
- Why it works: it flips the belief that adults need sheet music and years, and the proof is on screen before you finish the sentence.

**Two backups**
- Experimenter. Shot: her hands. Say: "My student's third lesson. Listen." Text: "Third lesson, real song."
- Teacher. Shot: four chord shapes, one at a time. Say: "Four shapes, and you can play this song." Text: "4 shapes, 1 real song."

**What I changed from your ask**
"Learn piano in 7 days" promises something your proof doesn't show and invites the comments you don't want. "Her third lesson" is true and more convincing.

**Test it**
Post the top pick. In your own Instagram insights, compare its 3-second views and average watch time with your last five Reels. No guess on numbers.

**Check these**
1. Your student is happy for her clip to open the video.
2. It really was her third lesson.
3. You have the four chord shapes filmed close enough to read on a phone.
