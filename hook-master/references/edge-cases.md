# Edge cases

## No footage yet, only an idea

Start from the key visual, not the words. Ask: what is the one frame that proves this? Find the contrast in it, pick a format, write the spoken line, then the text, then replay it in your head with sound off and eyes closed. If no frame can prove the claim, the idea isn't ready. Say so.

## Talking head only, nothing else to show

The face is the visual, so the words and text carry more. Use a change of shot in the first second (a closer crop, a step towards camera, an object held up), and put the topic in the text. Suggest one cheap insert shot they can film on a phone today.

## Paid ads

Same rules, plus the platform's ad policy. No before and after claims that break policy in health, finance or beauty. The hook must match the landing page, or the ad pays for clicks that bounce.

## Regulated fields (health, dental, legal, finance)

Describe what happens, not outcomes. No "painless", no "doesn't hurt", no "guaranteed", no "doctors hate this", no claims about what other professionals do or hide. Name the relevant regulator or advertising code under Check these rather than guessing its exact wording.

## They want a "secret" or "nobody tells you" hook

Only if it is genuinely little known and the video reveals it. Otherwise it is a false claim about other people and the sceptic swipes. Offer the true contrast instead: "Most people think [A]. It's [B]." with A from their comments, not from a made-up survey.

## They want a number in the hook and have none

Use their own number if they have one, labelled (yours). If not, use a visible quantity from the footage (minutes on a clock, the count of items in shot) or write [N] and put it under Check these. Never round up or invent.

## They ask which hook will go viral

Rank them and give the reason. Then say plainly that nobody can know which will go viral; the test tells you.

## They ask for 20 hooks

Give five or six in distinct formats, each with its first shot. Say why: twenty versions of one shape are one hook.

## Long-form (YouTube intro)

The hook must also pay off the title and thumbnail. Confirm their promise in the first sentence, then show why the next minutes are worth it. The lock-in can run a few sentences longer.
