# Failure examples

Made-up examples, written for this skill. Each pair fixes one failure without causing another.

## Delay

- Before: "Hey guys, welcome back to the channel. So a lot of you have been asking me about meal prep."
- After: "This is five lunches for the week, and it took 40 minutes." (on screen: the five boxes, lids off)
- Why: the subject and the result arrive in the first sentence and the picture shows them.

## Confusion

- Before: "This changes everything about how you think about it."
- After: "Your houseplant isn't dying from too little water. It's drowning."
- Why: "this" and "it" point at nothing. The fix names the subject and the surprise.

## Irrelevance

- Before: "Three years ago I opened my bakery with nothing but a dream."
- After: "If your croissants come out flat, it's usually the butter temperature, not the recipe."
- Why: the viewer's problem comes first. The bakery story can be the proof later.

## Disinterest

- Before: "Here are some tips for better sleep."
- After: "I stopped scrolling in bed for a week. The hard part wasn't the evenings."
- Why: an experiment with a twist creates a question. Only use it if the creator actually did it.

## When the picture rescues the words

- Spoken: "Okay, watch this." Visual: a 2-metre wave of foam pouring out of a washing machine.
- Not a delay failure: the picture carries the subject and the question at second zero.

## Overcorrected (a new failure)

- Before: "Today I'll explain how compound interest works."
- Overcorrected: "Money. Time. Magic." (fixes delay, causes confusion)
- Better: "Leaving [amount] alone for 30 years can grow more than adding to it in your 40s. Here's why." (only with real numbers from the creator, or as a [placeholder])
