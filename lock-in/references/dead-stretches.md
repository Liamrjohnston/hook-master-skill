# Dead stretches after the hook

These are the things that most often sit between a good hook and its payoff. Each one turns curiosity into "this was bait".

| Dead stretch | What to do |
|---|---|
| Introducing yourself after the hook | Move your name to the caption or a later line. Credentials go after the payoff, as proof |
| Backstory ("so last year I...") | Cut to the payoff. Keep one line of story only if it raises the stakes for the viewer |
| Setup lecture (theory, history, definitions) | Show the result first, explain after, and only as much as the viewer needs |
| "Before we start, follow for more" | Cut. Ask at the end, if at all |
| Repeating the hook in other words | Cut. The next line must add something |
| A slow walk, a pause, a sip of coffee | Cut on the first frame where something happens |

Rule of thumb: if the payoff the hook promised isn't visible or said within the next 1 to 3 sentences, something in this table is in the way.
